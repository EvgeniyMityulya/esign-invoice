Signing on a phone

How to sign a document on an iPhone

Three ways, from the one built into the phone to the one that works when someone else has to sign.

You do not need an app to sign a PDF yourself on an iPhone. You do need one the moment a client, a tenant or a subcontractor has to put their name on it while standing in front of you.

Signing something yourself, free, no app

  1. Open the PDF in Files or straight from the email
  2. Tap the markup icon, the pen tip in the top right
  3. Tap the plus, choose Signature, draw it once and it is saved for next time
  4. Drag it onto the line, resize, then share the file back

Where Markup runs out

Markup has no idea what a document is. It cannot record who signed, it cannot stamp a date next to the mark, and it treats a signature as a drawing on top of a picture. For your own paperwork that is fine. For anything you might have to defend later, it is thin.

When the other person signs

This is the real reason people look for an app. Inko keeps the document on your phone, you turn the screen around, and the client draws with a finger. No app for them, no account, no email round trip. The signed PDF and its record land back on your device.

What about a photo of a paper signature

It works and it is legal, but it is the weakest option. A pasted image carries no timestamp and no link to the document it sits on, so it is the easiest thing to dispute and the easiest to reuse without permission.

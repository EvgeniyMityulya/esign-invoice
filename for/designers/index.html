Who uses Inko

Invoices and estimates for designers

Rounds of revisions, usage rights and a deposit before the first sketch. The paperwork has to say all of that.

Design work goes wrong in a predictable place: the number of revisions and what the client is allowed to do with the result. Both belong on the estimate, in writing, signed, before anything gets designed.

A project through the app

  1. Estimate lists the deliverable, the number of rounds and the usage
  2. Deposit as its own line, so the terms are visible not verbal
  3. Client signs the estimate, on your screen or at the meeting
  4. Convert to an invoice on delivery, add extra rounds as new lines

Revisions as line items

Two rounds included, third round priced. Written that way on the estimate, an extra round becomes a normal conversation about a number rather than an argument about what was implied.

Documents that look like your studio

Three premium templates with your own accent colour, logo and typeface. A designer sending a default beige invoice undermines the invoice.

Deposits and staged payments

Split the job across documents, mark each paid as it lands, and see what is outstanding per client without opening a spreadsheet.
